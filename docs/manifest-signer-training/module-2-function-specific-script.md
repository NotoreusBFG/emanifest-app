## Scene 2 — What your signature legally certifies

**[VISUAL: Item 15's certification text on screen, with two clauses highlighted one at a time as narration reaches them]**

**NARRATION:**
"By signing Item 15, you're personally certifying two separate things.
First: that the shipment is fully and accurately described by proper
shipping name, and is classed, packed, marked, and labeled in accordance
with DOT regulations — specifically 49 CFR 172.204 and 172.205."

*Quiz tie-in (Q2): "Signing Item 15 certifies the shipment is properly classed, packed, marked, and labeled per which DOT sections?" — 172.204/172.205.*

**NARRATION (cont.):**
"Second — and this part depends on the generator's size. If the generator
is a Large Quantity Generator, signing also certifies that they have a
waste minimization program in place, under 40 CFR 262.27(a). Small
Quantity Generators certify a lighter-weight version of that same
statement, under 262.27(b)."

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT: LQG → 262.27(a) · SQG → 262.27(b)]**

*Quiz tie-in (Q3): "True/False: A Large Quantity Generator's Item 15 signature also certifies a waste minimization program under 40 CFR 262.27(a)." — True.*

## Scene 3 — Worked example: two generators, two signatures

**[VISUAL: Two side-by-side mock manifests, "Acme Plating (LQG)" and "Riverside Dental (SQG)"]**

**NARRATION:**
"Let's make that concrete. Acme Plating generates enough hazardous waste
each month to be a Large Quantity Generator. When their site manager signs
Item 15, they're certifying both the shipping description *and* the
262.27(a) waste minimization statement. Riverside Dental, a small quantity
generator down the street, signs the same box — but their certification is
the lighter 262.27(b) statement instead. Same box on the form. Different
legal statement, depending entirely on generator status."

## Scene 4 — Who is actually allowed to sign

**[VISUAL: An org chart — Owner/Operator at top, an "Authorized Representative" node beside it, a "Quick-Sign Delegate" node connected by a dotted line labeled "signs on behalf of"]**

**NARRATION:**
"The signer has to be someone authorized to bind the generator — the
owner, the operator, or an authorized representative. This matters a lot
if you're using ManifestMate's Quick-Sign delegation feature, for example
as a driver signing on a generator's behalf. When you sign as a delegate,
you are not making your own independent certification. You're signing on
behalf of the account owner — the legal certification is theirs, made
through you."

*Quiz tie-in (Q4): "If you sign as a delegate under a Quick-Sign delegation, whose certification are you legally making?" — the owner's/account holder's, not your own.*

## Scene 5 — What getting it wrong actually costs

**[VISUAL: A document with a red "FALSE STATEMENT" stamp, then a scale-of-justice icon]**

**NARRATION:**
"A knowing false statement on a document required to be maintained under
RCRA — and the manifest is exactly that — can carry both civil penalties
and criminal liability, under 42 U.S.C. section 6928(d), RCRA's criminal
enforcement provision. That's separate from the routine civil penalties
that apply to a late or incomplete manifest."

*Quiz tie-in (Q9): "What federal statute carries potential criminal liability for a knowing false statement on a RCRA-required document like the manifest?" — 42 U.S.C. §6928(d).*

**NARRATION (cont.):**
"We're deliberately not putting a dollar figure on screen here — EPA's
civil penalty amounts are adjusted for inflation every year, so a number
we recorded today could be wrong by the time you're watching this. Check
EPA's current civil penalty policy directly if you need the live figure."

## Scene 6 — Four common real-world signing mistakes

**[VISUAL: Four numbered cards, each flipping to reveal a short example as narration reaches it]**

**NARRATION:**
"Four mistakes we see often enough to call out specifically.

One: signing before the waste is actually loaded and ready. The date on
the manifest has to match the actual offer-for-transport date — not the
day you filled out the paperwork.

Two: signing for waste that's subject to Land Disposal Restrictions
without the required LDR notification paperwork attached.

Three: a delegate signing without an active, correctly-scoped Quick-Sign
delegation in place — if the delegation lapsed or was never scoped to
cover that site, the signature isn't valid.

Four: confusing the generator's certification block with the transporter's
or the designated facility's block. Only complete the block that matches
your actual role in that specific shipment."
