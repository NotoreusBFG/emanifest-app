# Module 3 — Security Awareness (video script, DRAFT)

Target length: ~8-10 min spoken. Satisfies 49 CFR 172.704(a)(4)/(c). Read
narration close to verbatim — regulatory citations should not be
paraphrased.

---

## Scene 1 — Cold open: the physical moment of handoff

**[VISUAL: A loaded pallet of labeled drums sitting on a loading dock, no truck yet in frame]**

**NARRATION:**
"Modules 1 and 2 were about paperwork. This one is about the physical
moment your waste actually leaves your custody — because DOT requires
security awareness training for exactly this handoff."

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT: 49 CFR 172.704(c) — Security awareness training]**

---

## Scene 2 — What the regulation actually asks for

**[VISUAL: Regulation text on screen, key phrase highlighted]**

**NARRATION:**
"172.704(c) requires — quote — 'an awareness of security risks associated
with hazardous materials transportation and methods designed to enhance
transportation security,' including how to recognize and respond to
possible security threats. In plain terms, for someone signing on the
generator side, that breaks down into a few concrete habits."

---

## Scene 3 — Habit one: don't leave it unattended

**[VISUAL: Time-lapse of a loading dock — waste staged, then a "door open, no one around" warning icon appears]**

**NARRATION:**
"Don't leave a loaded or staged hazmat shipment unattended, or accessible
to people who shouldn't be near it, before pickup. A staged shipment
sitting on an open dock overnight is a real, physical security gap — not
a hypothetical one."

---

## Scene 4 — Habit two: verify before you release custody

**[VISUAL: A driver approaching with a clipboard; a checklist appears — "Company matches? Driver ID matches? Expected today?"]**

**NARRATION:**
"Before you release custody of a shipment to a transporter, verify that
the driver and vehicle actually match who was expected — company, and
driver identification. Don't hand off a hazardous waste shipment to
whoever happens to show up."

*Quiz tie-in (Q8): "Before releasing a hazmat shipment to a transporter, what should you verify?" — that the driver/vehicle matches who was actually expected.*

---

## Scene 5 — Habit three: know who to call

**[VISUAL: Simple flowchart — "Something looks wrong" → arrow → "Who at your company?" → a phone icon]**

**NARRATION:**
"Know, in advance, who at your company to notify if something about a
pickup looks wrong — the wrong vehicle, an unscheduled pickup, a driver
who can't produce ID, or any sign of tampering with a container. This
should be a name you already know, not something you figure out in the
moment."

---

## Scene 6 — What this module does NOT cover

**[VISUAL: A red "not covered" banner over a padlocked "Security Plan" folder icon]**

**NARRATION:**
"One important boundary: this module does not cover in-depth,
company-level security-plan training under 172.802. That level of
training only applies if your company is required to have a formal DOT
security plan — a quantity and hazard-class threshold that most
generators never hit. If that applies to your company, you need separate,
dedicated training beyond this course."

*Quiz tie-in (Q10): "True/False: In-depth security training under 172.802 applies to every generator regardless of size." — False, only generators required to have a DOT security plan.*

---

## Scene 7 — Close: what this course did and didn't cover

**[VISUAL: A checklist with three items checked — General Awareness, Function-Specific, Security Awareness — and two greyed-out items — Safety Training, In-Depth Security]**

**NARRATION:**
"That's the course. To be direct about its scope: you've now covered
General Awareness, Function-Specific training for manifest signing, and
Security Awareness — three of DOT's five 172.704 components. This course
does not cover Safety Training, which by regulation has to reflect your
specific facility's materials and emergency response procedures — you
need to supplement with your own site-specific training for that. And it
doesn't cover In-Depth Security Training unless your company is required
to have a DOT security plan."

*Quiz tie-in (Q7): "True/False: This course satisfies DOT's safety-training component of 172.704." — False, safety training must be facility/material-specific.*
